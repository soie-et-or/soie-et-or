Sandro or IRO? Two Takes on Parisian Chic

Both are Parisian, both are loved, but they dress very different women. Here is how to tell which one is yours.

20 October 2026 · 5 min read

If you shop for Parisian ready-to-wear, two names come up again and again: Sandro and IRO. They sit at a similar level and are often found on the same rails, yet their mood is quite different. Knowing which one fits you saves a lot of returns.

Sandro: polished and feminine

Sandro dresses the Parisian woman on her way to work, to dinner, to a gallery opening. Think tailored blazers, tweed jackets, knitted cardigans with jewel buttons, pleated skirts and dresses that move well. The look is put together and a little romantic, with sharp tailoring underneath.

Choose Sandro if you like structure, you want pieces that work for the office as well as the weekend, and you enjoy a feminine detail such as a bow, a scalloped edge or a bit of lace.

IRO: relaxed with an edge

IRO was founded in Paris in 2005 by brothers Laurent and Arik Bitton, who came from the music industry. You can still feel that rock influence: leather jackets, slim jeans, slouchy knits, short dresses worn with boots. The look is cool and a little undone, the Parisian off-duty uniform.

Choose IRO if your wardrobe leans casual, you like leather and denim, and you want clothes that look better the more you wear them.

Fit and sizing

Both brands use French sizing. Check each product's size guide, and read the fit notes on the retailer's page. Fitted knits and jackets in particular can run small, so it is worth checking measurements rather than guessing.

Why not both?

The two mix well. A Sandro tweed jacket over IRO jeans and a white T-shirt is a very good outfit. Add loafers and one gold piece and you have the Parisian balance of polish and ease.
