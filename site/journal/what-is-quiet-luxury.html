What Quiet Luxury Really Means (and What It Does Not)

The term is everywhere. Underneath the trend is a simple, lasting idea about how to dress.

20 October 2026 · 4 min read

Style notes

Quiet luxury became a buzzword, which is a little ironic for something meant to be quiet. Strip away the headlines and it describes a way of dressing that Parisian women have practised for a long time: buy fewer things, buy better things, and let them speak softly.

What it is

  • Fabric first. Cashmere, silk, good wool, real leather. The quality shows in how a piece drapes and ages.
  • Fit that is right. A simple trouser that fits perfectly looks more expensive than an elaborate one that does not.
  • A narrow palette. Neutrals with an occasional deep colour. Everything combines.
  • Recognition through design, not logos. A Cartier Tank or a Pomellato ring is recognised by people who know, and invisible to people who do not. That is the point.

What it is not

  • It is not beige for the sake of beige. Colour is welcome when it is chosen.
  • It is not about price alone. A well-made piece from a smaller label can be quieter and more luxurious than a famous logo.
  • It is not a uniform you buy in one go. It is a wardrobe that grows slowly.

Where to start

Start with the pieces you wear most: a coat, a knit, a pair of trousers and one piece of jewelry you never take off. Upgrade those first. Our Parisian autumn capsule is a good map.
